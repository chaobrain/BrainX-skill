## Inspected data contract
- Data sources and inspected contents: Brunel (2000), DOI `10.1023/A:1008925309027`, especially Eqs. 1-2, the Model A parameter description, Section 6, Fig. 8 and Table 1. No empirical data or author simulation code were supplied.
- Shapes, axes, sampling/time base, and physical units: Use `NE=10000`, `NI=2500`, `CE=1000`, `CI=250`, `Cext=1000`, `tau_m=20 ms`, `tau_ref=2 ms`, threshold `20 mV`, reset `10 mV`, excitatory jump `J=0.1 mV`, inhibitory jump `-gJ`, and delay `1.5 ms`. Resolve time at `dt=0.1 ms`, matching the Fig. 8 global-rate bins. Store time-major Boolean probe rasters and full-population E/I spike counts after burn-in.
- Required preprocessing and the subset used to fit each transform: Discard a fixed burn-in before selecting a fixed displayed window. Convert spike counts to population rates in Hz using the 0.1 ms bin width. Compute firing rates, per-neuron ISI CV where at least four spikes are present, and a mean-centered Welch spectrum for frequency assessment. No transform is fit and no training occurs.
- Mapping from data to model inputs, targets, and observables: Each neuron receives exactly `CE` recurrent E and `CI` recurrent I sources plus the summed count of `Cext` independent Poisson inputs. Since `nu_thr = theta/(J*CE*tau_m) = 10 Hz`, the aggregate external count per 0.1 ms step has mean `Cext*eta*nu_thr*dt = eta`. Delayed presynaptic events produce voltage jumps. Observables are probe spikes, E/I/global counts and rates, firing rates, ISI CV, and global-rate spectral peaks.
- Known data limitations or unresolved mismatches: The paper does not publish its RNG, initial voltages, exact sampled neurons, or complete simulation duration, so pixel-identical reproduction is impossible. A finite stochastic realization can show weak oscillations in panel C, as the paper itself notes. The external afferents are represented by their mathematically equivalent summed Poisson count rather than 12.5 million explicit input synapses.
