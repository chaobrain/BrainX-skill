# NeuroSpecification

- Status: locked
- Researcher approval: Approved on 2026-08-24 with the proposed 0.1 ms timestep, 500 ms burn-in, 2,000 ms analysis, five fixed-seed repeats, and prospective classification criteria unchanged.

## Researcher request
- Brain-modeling question or behavior: Do the four Brunel model-A parameter points reproduce synchronous regular, fast synchronous irregular, asynchronous irregular, and slow synchronous irregular population states?
- Requested model, experiment, or comparison: Simulate 10,000 excitatory and 2,500 inhibitory LIF neurons at `(g, eta) = (3, 2), (6, 4), (5, 2), (4.5, 0.9)` with fixed indegree, delayed voltage jumps, and independent external Poisson drive; compare the measured state at each point with the corresponding published regime.
- Execution mode: forward-simulation
- Required outputs: Per-condition raster, population rate, E/I firing rates, E/I per-neuron ISI CV, population-rate power spectrum, dominant population frequency, per-repeat metrics, aggregate robustness summary, exact configuration, and fixed seeds.
- Constraints: Use BrainPy-State for point neurons, BrainEvent for sparse event-driven projections, BrainUnit for voltage and time, and BrainState for State-aware compiled execution. Use model A with `N_E = 10000`, `N_I = 2500`, `epsilon = 0.1`, exact indegrees `C_E = 1000` and `C_I = 250`, `C_ext = C_E = 1000`, `tau_m = 20 ms`, `theta = 20 mV`, `V_reset = 10 mV`, `tau_ref = 2 ms`, `J = 0.1 mV`, inhibitory jump `-g J`, external jump `J`, and delay `D = 1.5 ms`. Use `nu_thr = theta / (J C_E tau_m) = 10 Hz` per external synapse and `nu_ext = eta nu_thr`. Proposed numerical protocol: `dt = 0.1 ms`, 500 ms burn-in, 2,000 ms analyzed duration, five independent fixed seeds, one fixed-indegree graph and independent initial/external randomness per seed shared across the four parameter conditions.

## Inspected data contract
- Data sources and inspected contents: No empirical data were supplied. The model and four parameter points match Brunel (2000), Figure 8 and Table 1; the paper supplies the equations, canonical parameters, expected qualitative regimes, and reference rates/frequencies for the three inhibition-dominated conditions.
- Shapes, axes, sampling/time base, and physical units: Each run has 12,500 neurons split into E then I populations. Simulation time uses milliseconds and voltage uses millivolts. Proposed `dt = 0.1 ms` makes the 1.5 ms delay exactly 15 steps. Population activity is retained in 0.1 ms bins; presentation and synchronization metrics may additionally use explicitly labeled 1 ms bins. Raster output uses the same fixed 50 E and 50 I neuron indices in every condition and repeat.
- Required preprocessing and the subset used to fit each transform: Discard the first 500 ms before every metric. Remove the population-rate mean before spectral estimation. Compute Welch spectra from the full analyzed interval without inspecting outcomes to change bands or thresholds. No fitting or training is performed.
- Mapping from data to model inputs, targets, and observables: Each neuron receives independent aggregate Poisson counts with mean `C_ext nu_ext dt`; recurrent E and I spikes arrive after 1.5 ms through exact fixed-indegree projections. Observables are E/I spike trains, E/I mean firing rates, the mean per-neuron ISI CV among neurons with at least three intervals, population spike counts/rates, Welch power, dominant non-DC frequency, peak-to-background power, and 1 ms population-rate CV.
- Known data limitations or unresolved mismatches: Finite networks can show weak damped spectral peaks in the asynchronous state. A 2 s analysis gives limited frequency resolution and relatively few intervals for very-low-rate individual neurons, mitigated by 12,500 neurons and five repeats. Aggregate per-neuron external Poisson counts preserve the summed external input law but do not instantiate 1,000 explicit external source neurons per target. Fixed-indegree graphs are independently sampled by repeat and held fixed across conditions within a repeat.

## Acceptance boundary
- Evidence required for success, failure, or an inconclusive result: Classify a repeat as regular when pooled E/I mean ISI CV is below 0.5 and irregular when it is at least 0.7. Classify population activity as synchronous only when both its 1 ms rate CV is at least 0.2 and its dominant spectral peak is at least five times the median power outside a +/-5 Hz exclusion around the peak. A synchronous irregular repeat is fast at 100-300 Hz and slow at 10-60 Hz, with mean neuron rate below dominant population frequency. Classify AI only when firing is irregular and the full synchrony predicate is false. Values between regularity thresholds or outside frequency bands are inconclusive. A requested regime is robust only when at least four of five repeats satisfy its complete predicate and the aggregate median does too.
- Required baselines and controls: Verify exact indegree and weight signs, the 15-step delay, external-rate conversion, fixed graph reuse across conditions within each seed, independent reset between runs, deterministic replay for one seed, finite State and outputs, a no-recurrence external-drive check, and small-network eager/compiled parity. Compare fast-SI, AI, and slow-SI rates/frequencies with the published simulation values 60.7/180 Hz, 37.7/no sustained oscillation, and 5.5/22 Hz without using those values to force labels.
- Invalid-result conditions: Variable indegree, duplicate edges where sampling is specified without replacement, autapses, wrong E/I source counts, delayed or refractory State carried across independent runs, external drive scaled as one rather than 1,000 synapses, unit mismatch, omitted burn-in, post-outcome threshold changes, missing repeats, non-finite metrics, or a regime label based only on the largest FFT bin.
- Allowed claims and explicit non-claims: Report whether each sampled parameter point robustly satisfies the prospective finite-network predicates and whether its observables qualitatively reproduce Brunel's published state. Do not claim a continuous phase boundary, biological realism, exact replication of the original random graph or random-number generator, or absence of finite-size oscillatory structure in AI.
